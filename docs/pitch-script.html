Speaker 1

The hook

Open cold. No greeting, no "good morning respected judges". Walk to the centre and ask the room a question.

Say
Ask any one of us where we live, and we'll say — flat 502, fifth floor, Ganga Residency. Now ask the government the same question. The land record can only answer with a plot number. It knows the ground. It has never known the floor.
Analogy — the postman
Imagine a sixty-flat building where every single family has the same postal address. The postman arrives with sixty letters and no way to tell them apart. He would have to guess.
The tie-back: that is not a thought experiment. That is precisely how India's land record sees that building today — one plot, one entry, sixty families.
Then land it
Our cities grew upward. Our land records stayed flat.

Delivery

Pause a full second after "stayed flat". That silence is what turns a statement into a hook. Do not rush into the next slide.

The problem statement

FILL THIS IN before printing — PS ID, exact official title, ministry or organisation, and category (software / hardware).

Read the official title once, verbatim. Judges are cross-checking that you are answering the statement you were assigned, and hearing the exact words settles that in five seconds.

Problem statement [ID] — [exact official title], from [ministry]. In plain language: extend India's land parcel identification system into the third dimension.

Why the plain-language restatement matters

Official problem statements are written in policy language. Immediately translating it shows you understood the ask rather than memorised it — and it sets up the next ninety seconds.

What is actually broken

This is the beat where judges either understand the project or spend the rest of the pitch confused. Build the column up on screen, band by band, while you speak.

Say, while the bands appear
Here is one plot in any Indian city. A metro tunnel fifteen metres down. A water main above it. Two levels of parking. Shops at ground. Eleven floors of flats. Air rights on top. Four different authorities, dozens of owners — and every one of them sits at the same ground coordinate.
Analogy — the multi-storey car park
It is a multi-storey car park where the ticket only says “car park”. Your car is definitely in the building. Which level? The ticket has no way to tell you.
The tie-back: the ticket isn't wrong — it is incomplete by design. It was built for a world with one level. That is our land record.

What that costs — three real consequences

Give one sentence each. Do not elaborate; the demo will show them.

  • Disputes have no geometry. When two neighbours argue over an enclosed balcony, there is no authoritative 3D boundary to settle it against — so it goes to court, for years.
  • Utilities get cut. A builder excavating a basement has no machine-readable record of what runs beneath. Every city has a steady rate of struck water mains and cables.
  • Unauthorised floors are invisible. If a builder adds a twelfth floor to an eleven-floor sanction, the file still agrees with itself perfectly. Nothing in the record can contradict it.
Close the beat
A two-dimensional identifier can name the column. It cannot name anything inside it.

Speaker 2
On screen: a railway ticket, then the ULPIN structure beside it

What the statement actually requires

Do not list the requirements as bullets off the official PDF. Judges have read it. Show that you understood why each one is there.

Analogy — the railway berth
A train ticket does not just say “coach S7”. It says coach S7, berth 42, upper. Remove that one word — upper — and three passengers have a valid claim to the same space.
The tie-back: Indian Railways solved the vertical problem decades ago, for a berth. We are being asked to solve it for a building. The plot is the coach. The flat is the berth. The floor is lower, middle or upper.

Four things the statement asks for, and the one-line reason for each:

RequirementWhy it is there
A 3D identifier — X, Y and Z Without the Z, the flat and the tunnel under it are the same record.
Automated extraction from drone imagery and LiDAR Hand-digitising every building in India is not a plan; it is a wish.
Volumetric parcels above and below ground Air rights are traded and tunnels are dug. Both need a legal extent.
Topology validation A register nobody checks accumulates errors until it is worthless.
Bridge into the solution
So: give every legally meaningful volume of urban space its own berth number. That is what we built.

On screen: the one-line equation, then the ULPIN broken into fields

Bhoomi3D — the solution

This is the most important beat in the pitch

Everything else can be recovered from. If judges leave this section without understanding how it works, the demo becomes a light show. Slow down here. Teach one idea, completely.

Start with the equation

Put this on screen and say it out loud. It is the whole system.

Say
A shape on a plan, plus which floor it is on, plus how tall that floor is — equals a property you can measure, own and point at. Everything else we built sits on top of that one line.

Then show the identifier

Analogy — the PIN code that ran out of digits
A PIN code narrows you to a locality. Add a street, a building, a flat number and you have a delivery address. Each extra field removes ambiguity that the field before it could not.
The tie-back: our 3D-ULPIN does the same thing, downward and upward. State, district, tehsil, then position on the ground, then which stratum — above ground, below ground, air rights — then which level, then which unit.

Put the number on screen, broken into its parts, and walk it left to right:

PartMeans
27 · 025 · 004Maharashtra, Pune, Pune City — the existing government codes
tek92et7bxwhere it stands, to about a metre — readable with no database
B04the new part — above ground, fourth floor
00DYwhich unit on that floor
5a check character
Say — and this line earns real credibility
That last character is a checksum. If a clerk mistypes any single character, or swaps two next to each other, the number is rejected offline — before it ever reaches a database. We tested that exhaustively: all nine hundred and ten possible single-character errors, every one caught.

If you have thirty seconds spare, add this

A ULPIN is minted once and never changes. If a re-survey moves a boundary by three centimetres, the number stays — otherwise every deed referencing that property would be orphaned. We report the drift as an advisory instead. Judges who work with records will notice you thought about this.

The demo

Before you walk in

Dataset built, server started, browser already open on the loaded viewer. Never build live. Check no old server is running — a leftover process serves a stale register and you will be demoing yesterday's data without knowing.

Six moves, in this order

  1. Open on the register. Top bar reads 143 objects · 97 units · 30 storeys · 143 ULPINs · z −17.0 to 68.2 m.
    Say: “Nothing about these buildings was given to the software. It was handed a point cloud and worked them out.”
  2. Click a building, a floor, then a flat. The identifier appears decoded, with area, volume, height range, owner, and a provenance badge.
    Say: “That badge says this boundary came from an approved plan — not from our guess. Every object records where its boundary came from.”
  3. Column probe — click the ground. Twenty-nine objects appear in one vertical stack.
    Say: “Twenty-nine legal objects at one coordinate. A 2D record has one row for this.”
  4. Validation tab. Walk the three defects. Press show in 3D — the camera flies to the problem and outlines it in red.
  5. 2D → 3D tab. Upload a plan file, show the floor drawing, press build. A new register in under a second.
    Say: “This is the path a municipal office can run today, with plans they already hold. No drone required.”
  6. Re-run AI pipeline. Eight stages execute live and stream back in about seventeen seconds. Always end here.
Analogy — the X-ray and the case file
A doctor does not choose between your case file and your X-ray. The file says what should be there. The X-ray shows what is. When they disagree, that disagreement is the diagnosis.
Use this when you show the unauthorised storey: the approved plan sanctions eleven floors. The drone scan measures twelve. The paper record agreed with itself perfectly — only an independent measurement could catch it. That is roughly 2,700 cubic metres of unregistered construction, and we found it in seconds.

The strongest single moment

Pressing Re-run AI pipeline and letting eight stages tick past live proves it is a working system rather than a rendered mock-up. Nothing else you can do in ten minutes is as convincing. Save it for last and do not talk over it.

If asked "where is the deep learning?"

What we added beyond the brief

This beat separates a completed assignment from a product. Name them fast — one line each.

What we addedWhy it matters
2D plan upload path Municipal offices hold approved plans, not LiDAR. This is the path they can run today — no drone, no GPU, under a second.
Self-scoring pipeline Every stage reports measured accuracy on every run. Nothing else in the field does this.
Provenance on every object Surveyed, approved, or machine-inferred. A record with legal weight must never present a guess as a measurement.
Two-sensor cross-check Imagery and LiDAR extracted independently, then matched. Two sensors agreeing is evidence; one repeating itself is not.
Unauthorised-construction detection Not in the brief. Falls out of comparing as-built against sanctioned — and it is what a municipal commissioner will care about most.
Offline by design No CDN, no API keys, no internet at runtime. It runs in a tehsil office with no connectivity.
Analogy — spell-check
Nobody asked for spell-check. It was added because a document full of errors nobody catches is worse than no document — and now writing without it feels reckless.
The tie-back: our validator is spell-check for land records. Ten rules, running over the whole register, catching overlapping ownership, unclaimed space and clearance breaches before they become litigation.

Rules for the room

  • One speaker per beat, no interruptions. Handovers should be silent — walk, don't announce.
  • Never read the slide aloud. The slide is the evidence; you are the argument.
  • Numbers slowly, adjectives never. "Nought point nine eight four" lands. "Highly accurate" does not.
  • If a demo step fails, keep moving. Say what it would have shown, continue, come back if there's time. Do not debug on stage.
  • Everyone answers questions. Judges often aim at whoever hasn't spoken. "That's his part" undoes everything already said.

Timing discipline

Ten minutes is the target; the demo is the compressible part, not the explanation. If you are running late at 5:55, cut demo steps 4 and 5 and keep 1, 2, 3 and 6. Never cut the solution beat at 3:55 — a judge who does not understand the idea cannot be impressed by the demo.

The three questions most likely to come

1. “Your data is synthetic.” Lead with it before they raise it: synthetic so that ground truth exists — otherwise accuracy is an opinion. The pipeline reads real LAS files today.
2. “Where is the AI?” Name the algorithms, cite ISPRS, give the legal-auditability reason.
3. “Have you spoken to a land-records department?” Do not bluff. Say what you grounded the design in — LGD codes, existing ULPIN structure, DILRMP, SVAMITVA — and state the pilot as the next step.
